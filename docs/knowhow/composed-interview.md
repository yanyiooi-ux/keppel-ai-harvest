# Composed interview: TS-CC (repeat comfort complaints on an office floor)

> **Composed from public sources, not a real interview. No real person said these words. Every answer restates a rule in `pill-v0-source-pack.md`, and every rule cites its checked sources.**

Used by the capture demo (prompt P11). If a real interview happens, its transcript replaces this file.

**Interviewer:** When a tenant calls to say a floor is too cold, what should the helpdesk ask first?

**Composed veteran:** Safety first. Any burning smell, smoke, water or a wet ceiling? Is anyone feeling unwell, or is there a chemical smell? Then where exactly, at what times, and how many people feel it. Then what has changed lately.

**Interviewer:** Why ask whether people are wearing jackets?

**Composed veteran:** Because cold people often keep quiet. They put on a jacket or move away from the vent. So cold complaints get under-counted.

**Interviewer:** What if someone mentions a burning smell?

**Composed veteran:** Then it is not a comfort job any more. Leave the unit off. Do not turn it back on to test it. Note where the smell was strongest and call the control room. For real smoke or fire, follow the building's fire plan and the fire wardens, and never take the lift.

**Interviewer:** And if someone is feeling unwell?

**Composed veteran:** If someone faints or cannot breathe properly, call 995 for an ambulance and give first aid. And remember, after a new fit-out, 'stuffy' can be fumes from new paint or carpet, not heat.

**Interviewer:** You find a heater under a desk. What do you do?

**Composed veteran:** Look, do not touch. If the plug or cord looks scorched or melted, keep people away and get the licensed electrical worker. If it looks normal, note it, note where it sits, and tell the tenant contact. A heater near the wall sensor can fool it, so the neighbour ends up colder.

**Interviewer:** Before anyone walks the floor, what do you look at?

**Composed veteran:** The trend. Zone temperature, setpoint, damper, airflow and supply air over a couple of weeks. One reading at the desk shows one moment. The trend shows the pattern.

**Interviewer:** Anything you check before you trust that trend?

**Composed veteran:** Leftover overrides. Points left in hand mode, forced dampers, setpoint changes nobody can explain, controllers offline. And the alarm log, because a fire alarm test can leave an air handler off.

**Interviewer:** How do you read the dampers?

**Composed veteran:** Put every box on that air handler on one chart. If most are near fully open, that is a bad sign, so look at the air handler too. If one box sits near fully open all day while the others are mid-range, look at that box, its duct and its sensor.

**Interviewer:** What changes if the floor had a fit-out?

**Composed veteran:** Pull the fit-out file. The layout plan shows where each box and thermostat should be. Get the commissioning report and the air balancing report, and check every box was switched back on. Before you repair anything the fit-out put in, find out who owns it and whether it is still under the contractor's defects period.

**Interviewer:** Cold mornings near the lift core. Where do you start?

**Composed veteran:** The start-up settings first: when pre-cooling starts and the overnight setpoint. Then see if the core box sits at its minimum airflow while the room stays below setpoint. That points to a minimum set too high, or a flow sensor out of calibration. But the minimum may also carry the fresh air, so check the fresh air design before lowering it, and check CO2 after.

**Interviewer:** Why not just raise the supply air temperature?

**Composed veteran:** Warmer supply air takes out less moisture, so rooms can turn clammy. And it changes the air for every zone on that air handler, maybe other floors too.

**Interviewer:** On the floor, what comes first?

**Composed veteran:** Agree a time with the tenant contact. Do not walk into a boardroom during a meeting. Then the sensor. Is it in the room it controls, wired to the right box, away from sun, vents and monitors? Does it match a calibrated handheld? An offset is only temporary. A sensor that has drifted gets replaced.

**Interviewer:** And the hot west boardroom in the afternoon?

**Composed veteran:** Suspect the sun first. Check the sun on people and glass, and the blinds. Sunny glass can make people hot even when the air reads normal. Check there is a return grille or a transfer path out of the room, and take a CO2 reading with the tenant's OK.

**Interviewer:** When do you go into the ceiling?

**Composed veteran:** Only after the screen and floor checks. Rope off the area below or do it after hours. Use a platform, not a leaning ladder. Isolate, lock off and prove it dead before touching a controller. Never go up alone.

**Interviewer:** What should the tenant be told while all this happens?

**Composed veteran:** Thank them, say what happens next and who will come back to them. Give only the approved hands-off tips. No cause, no blame, no price, no promised date.
