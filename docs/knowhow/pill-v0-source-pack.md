# Pill v0 source pack: TS-CC, repeat comfort complaints on an office floor

The know-how the pill is built from. Made 5 Oct 2026 by research agents: 8 researchers each covered one angle, a checker opened every source (144 items, 88 supported as written, 56 narrowed to what the page really says, 0 dropped), then a Singapore chief-engineer critic reviewed the draft and a strict checker tested the revision. **No real expert has been interviewed.** Every rule says where it comes from:

- **public source**: a checked page (links in `sources.md`).
- **practitioner opinion**: forums and trade writing, weaker than a standard.
- **team decision**: chosen in the approved plan (D6, D9), not from a source.
- **critic correction**: changed after the critic's safety review.

Comfort numbers from the 1996 NEA guideline are marked so and must be checked against the current SS 554. The app never quotes comfort ranges, prices or blame to a tenant. The same content, machine-readable: `pill-v0-source-pack.json`.

## Summary

Final pill v0 source pack for the Technical Services case: repeat comfort complaints (too cold, then too hot) on Level 23 of a Singapore Grade A office tower. All buildings, tenants and tickets are made up. It starts from the draft pack and applies all 9 of the critic's unsafe-or-wrong fixes, all 12 weak-rule fixes, and the critic's safety and demo gaps: health questions at intake, a 995 red stop for someone unwell, an ownership and defects-period check, permission to enter tenant space, isolate and lock off before box or AHU work, box minimums that also carry fresh air, temporary sensor offsets, a standing rule for what the AI may say to tenants, and six new golden tests. The repeat rule matches team decision D6. Colours, approvers and the never-list match team decision D9, including one green setpoint nudge of at most 1 degree C on a first complaint. v1.0 does not block that nudge after a fit-out. That block is held back as the reserved v1.1 rule (team decision D10). Every fact cites a research item or the critic. Comfort numbers from 1996 are labelled '1996 NEA guideline, check current SS 554', and the AI never quotes comfort ranges, prices or blame to tenants. The composed interview restates only source-backed rules and is not a real expert interview.

## Rules (26)

### TS-CC-01. The helpdesk asks three intake questions at the first call. Q1, safety and health: Is there any burning smell, smoke, water dripping or a wet ceiling? Is anyone feeling unwell (headache, sore eyes, dizzy, short of breath), or is there a chemical smell? Q2: Where exactly, at what times, and how many people feel it? Are people wearing jackets, using fans or moving desks? Q3: What has changed lately? New walls or rooms, new equipment, heaters or fans, after-hours use?

- **Colour:** green. **Who says yes:** No approval needed. Helpdesk does it.. **Section:** helpdesk intake. **Label:** mixed.
- **Because:** Singapore's WSH Council says to start by asking what people feel, how many are affected, where, and any symptoms (HC07). EPA says to look for time patterns and recent changes such as renovation (HC03). Cold people often keep quiet and put on a jacket, so cold complaints are under-counted (PC-17, HC10). After a new fit-out, 'stuffy' can mean fumes from new paint, carpet and furniture, not heat (critic).
- **Applies when:** Every comfort complaint, at the first contact.
- **Not when:** An emergency is already happening. Then follow the red stops first (TS-CC-03, TS-CC-04). A chemical smell with nobody badly unwell goes to the FM chief engineer for an air quality check before any comfort fix (critic).
- **Sources:** [HC07](https://www.tal.sg/wshc/-/media/tal/wshc/resources/publications/wsh-guidelines/files/wsh_guidelines_on_management_of_iaq.pdf), [HC03](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2014-08/documents/sec_6.pdf), [SG-03](https://policy.thinkbluedata.com/sites/default/files/Guidelines%20for%20good%20indoor%20air%20quality%20in%20office%20premises.pdf), [PC-17](https://www.sgbc.sg/beyond-the-setpoint-go-25/), [HC10](https://www.sgbc.sg/beyond-the-setpoint-go-25/), critic

### TS-CC-02. What the AI may say to a tenant. It may thank them, say what happens next and who will come back to them, ask the three intake questions, and give only the approved hands-off tips. It never gives a cause, blame, a price, a promised date or a comfort temperature range, and never tells the tenant to change building settings. Every AI reply is a draft until a person sends it.

- **Colour:** amber. **Who says yes:** The building manager approves routine explanations. The Asset Operations Manager writes or approves any message about cost, goodwill or fault (team decision D9).. **Section:** helpdesk intake. **Label:** critic correction.
- **Because:** The draft pack said 'no prices, no blame' only inside one rule and one test. The AI also must not diagnose to the tenant ('your fit-out caused this'), promise a date, or tell the tenant to change the building (critic). The comfort numbers we hold come from 1996 and may be out of date, so the AI should not quote comfort ranges to tenants at all (critic). SS 554 itself says meeting the code may still not suit everyone (SG-12).
- **Applies when:** Any AI-drafted message to a tenant, from the first acknowledgement to the follow-up.
- **Not when:** Messages between building staff. Those still follow the never-list (TS-CC-06).
- **Sources:** critic, [SG-12](https://www.singaporestandardseshop.sg/Product/GetPdf?fileName=180331105407SS+554-2016_Preview%28vA1382715%29.pdf&pdtid=8ee48ab1-38f5-4dae-a469-b8612a05876f)

### TS-CC-03. If a fire alarm goes off, or anyone reports smoke, flame, a burning or electrical smell, a scorched or melted plug or cord, a socket or heater cord that someone says is warm, or water dripping near lights, power boxes, electrical or comms rooms, stop the comfort job. It is now a safety job. Leave the equipment off. Do not turn it back on to test it. Keep away from the electrics. Do not switch or isolate circuits yourself unless you are authorised. For smoke or fire, follow the building's fire emergency plan and the Fire Safety Manager or fire wardens. Never use the lifts. The AI shows only short fixed text approved by the FM chief engineer.

- **Colour:** red. **Who says yes:** No one approves going ahead. Call the FM control room, the duty FM chief engineer and the Fire Safety Manager. The building LEW handles electrical faults. Call SCDF on 995 for active fire or heavy smoke.. **Section:** safety. **Label:** mixed.
- **Because:** After a burning smell, leave the system off and do not restart it to see if it happens again (R06). Check plugs and sockets by eye, and call an electrician for charring or damage (R03). Electrical work in commercial premises must be done or supervised by a Licensed Electrical Worker (R13). Keep away from electrics near water (R08). In a fire, do not use any lift (R07). A Singapore high-rise office has a Fire Safety Manager and its own evacuation plan, so the AI must not make up its own evacuation order (critic). The FM control room arranges isolation through the LEW (critic). A fire alarm going off is a red stop (team decision D9).
- **Applies when:** At helpdesk intake or on the floor, any sign of fire, electrical damage, or water near electrics.
- **Not when:** The smell clearly comes from outside, such as cooking, and nothing points to equipment. Even then, log it.
- **Sources:** [R03](https://ehs.mit.edu/campus-services-program/space-heater-use-guidelines/), [R06](https://aboveallheatingandair.com/ac-smells-like-burning-what-it-means), [R07](https://www.scdf.gov.sg/home/community-volunteers/fire-emergency-guides/fire-outbreak-in-a-high-rise-building), [R08](https://www.ohio.edu/safety/emergency-procedures/flooding-water-damage), [R13](https://www.i2r.com.sg/the-complete-guide-to-licensed-electrical-worker-lew-requirements-in-singapore-certification-responsibilities-compliance/), critic

### TS-CC-04. If someone is hurt, faints, has chest pain, or cannot breathe properly, stop everything else. Call 995 for an ambulance, give first aid, and tell the building control room.

- **Colour:** red. **Who says yes:** No one approves. Call SCDF on 995, the building first aider or CERT, and the FM control room.. **Section:** safety. **Label:** mixed.
- **Because:** The draft pack had red stops for fire, electrics and water, but none for a person who collapses or cannot breathe (critic). Singapore's WSH Council says complaint intake should ask about symptoms (HC07). Someone hurt, such as after a fall from a ladder, is a red stop too (team decision D9).
- **Applies when:** At intake or on the floor, any person who is hurt or badly unwell.
- **Not when:** Mild symptoms such as a headache or sore eyes with a chemical smell, and nobody in danger. Then the FM chief engineer arranges an air quality check before any comfort fix (TS-CC-01).
- **Sources:** critic, [HC07](https://www.tal.sg/wshc/-/media/tal/wshc/resources/publications/wsh-guidelines/files/wsh_guidelines_on_management_of_iaq.pdf)

### TS-CC-05. Never bypass a smoke detector trip, wedge a fire damper, or override an AHU fire shutdown to get air back. Never add, bleed or release refrigerant. Never open distribution boards or do mains-side wiring. Hand these jobs to the licensed or certified people through the FM chief engineer.

- **Colour:** red. **Who says yes:** FM chief engineer, with the Fire Safety Manager (fire systems), a certified chiller technician (refrigerant) or the building LEW (electrical).. **Section:** safety. **Label:** mixed.
- **Because:** Singapore's Fire Code needs return-air smoke detectors that shut down larger AHUs, and fire dampers where ducts cross compartment walls and floors (R16). Refrigerant work on water-cooled chillers must be done or supervised by a certified chiller technician from a registered company (NEA, from October 2022) (R14, critic). Electrical work in commercial premises must be done or supervised by an LEW (R13). US EPA rules ban venting refrigerant on purpose (R15).
- **Applies when:** An AHU keeps tripping, a fire damper seems shut, the trend points to the chiller or refrigerant, or an electrical fault turns up during the job.
- **Not when:** Normal VAV damper control that has nothing to do with fire or smoke dampers, or air-side checks on sensors, boxes and zoning.
- **Sources:** [R16](https://www.scdf.gov.sg/fire-safety-services-listing/fire-code-2023/table-of-content/chapter-7-mechanical-ventilation-smoke-control-systems/clause-7.1-air-conditioning-and-mechanical-ventilation-systems), [R14](https://www.nea.gov.sg/media/news/news/index/launch-of-course-on-chiller-refrigerant-handling), [R15](https://www.epa.gov/section608/managing-refrigeration-and-ac-equipment), [R13](https://www.i2r.com.sg/the-complete-guide-to-licensed-electrical-worker-lew-requirements-in-singapore-certification-responsibilities-compliance/), critic

### TS-CC-06. Never-list, for everyone and for the AI. Never quote a price or a dollar amount. Never admit fault or liability in writing. AI drafts never do this, and the Asset Operations Manager writes any such wording personally. Never show one tenant's data to another tenant. Never write to the BMS or any building system. The AI has no way to change a setting. A person makes every change.

- **Colour:** red. **Who says yes:** No one can approve an exception.. **Section:** safety. **Label:** team decision.
- **Because:** This is the team's line for what the AI may never do (team decision D9). It is checked in code, outside the model, so a prompt cannot talk past it. The critic's review confirms the AI never writes, prices or blames, and asks for a test where a technician asks the AI to push a setpoint to the BMS (critic).
- **Applies when:** Always.
- **Not when:** Never relaxed. A person may still make an approved change by hand under the other rules.
- **Sources:** critic

### TS-CC-07. Treat a personal heater on the floor as a safety finding and a clue. Look, do not touch. If the plug, cord or socket looks scorched, melted or discoloured, or there is a burning smell, use TS-CC-03. Otherwise note the heater and where it sits, check if it is near the zone sensor, and tell the tenant contact. Log it as a sign the tenant may have stopped trusting the building, and tell the building manager. The FM chief engineer decides on removal under the building's house rules.

- **Colour:** amber. **Who says yes:** FM chief engineer decides on removal or follow-up. The building manager is told.. **Section:** safety. **Label:** mixed.
- **Because:** Space heaters used wrongly can cause fire or electric shock (R05), and heaters on power strips can overheat the strip (R01). A heater near a shared thermostat can fool it, so the neighbour ends up much colder (R04, PT-06). Personal heaters are often banned as a fire risk (PT-07). Touching a possibly faulty item to judge its heat is unsafe, and a heater is tenant property, so removing it is a tenant-relations decision (critic).
- **Applies when:** A heater or foot warmer is seen on the floor walk, or reported at intake.
- **Not when:** No personal heater is in use in the zone. Any sign of damage or burning goes straight to TS-CC-03.
- **Sources:** [R01](https://www.ocwr.gov/publications/fast-facts/portable-space-heaters/), [R04](https://my.ece.ncsu.edu/facilities/hvac), [R05](https://www.travelers.com/resources/business-topics/workplace-safety/space-heater-safety-in-the-workplace), [PT-06](https://my.ece.ncsu.edu/facilities/hvac/), [PT-07](https://www.facilitiesnet.com/hvac/article/HotCold-Call-Burnout-IFMA-Study-Examines-Common-Worker-Complaints-Facilities-Management-HVAC-News--11026), critic

### TS-CC-08. Anything above the ceiling or on the AHU is planned work. Do screen and floor checks first. Ceiling work needs a named yes under the building's ceiling-access and work-at-height rules. Rope off the area below, or work after hours. Use a platform ladder or mobile platform, not a leaning ladder, for box work. No one goes up alone. Before touching a VAV controller or actuator, or opening an AHU door, isolate, lock off and prove it dead. Mains-side work is done by or under the LEW. Never climb under a wet ceiling near live fittings. Change filters outside working hours with the system off and with PPE. Replace wet ceiling tiles. Do not dry and reuse them.

- **Colour:** amber. **Who says yes:** FM chief engineer, or whoever issues the building's ceiling-access permit. The LEW for any mains-side work.. **Section:** safety. **Label:** mixed.
- **Because:** Singapore's Work at Heights rules call for safe access, a fall prevention plan, a permit-to-work system and supervision (R11). The permit-to-work rule is a legal must only in factories, but general work-at-height duties apply to all workplaces (SG-18, R10). The WSH (Work at Heights) Regulations 2013 and the WSH Council Code of Practice for Working Safely at Heights are the main public backing (critic). Ladders are still linked to workplace deaths in Singapore (R12). VAV controllers are commonly fed from 230 V, and mains-side work is LEW work (critic, R13). NEA sets the filter-change precautions (R18). Wet tiles should be thrown away to stop mould (R09).
- **Applies when:** Any step that needs a ladder, a platform or lifted ceiling tiles, such as a box flow test or an actuator check, and any AHU or filter work.
- **Not when:** Checks from the BMS screen or at floor level. Do those first.
- **Sources:** [R11](https://www.mom.gov.sg/newsroom/parliament-questions-and-replies/2026/0112-written-answer-to-pq-on--fall-from-height-safety-lapses-and-work-at-height-safeguards), [SG-18](https://www.mom.gov.sg/~/media/mom/documents/safety-health/factsheet%20on%20wahamendmentregulations.pdf), [R10](https://www.tal.sg/wshc/topics/work-at-height/preventing-falls-from-heights), [R12](https://www.tal.sg/wshc/resources/newsletters/wsh-advisory/workplace-fatalities-involving-sand-ladders-lightning-and-machines-in-1h2026), [R13](https://www.i2r.com.sg/the-complete-guide-to-licensed-electrical-worker-lew-requirements-in-singapore-certification-responsibilities-compliance/), [R18](https://www.nea.gov.sg/our-services/public-cleanliness/environmental-cleaning-guidelines/advisories/guidance-on-improving-ventilation-and-indoor-air-quality-in-buildings-for-a-healthy-indoor-environment), [R09](https://epa.gov/mold/mold-remediation-schools-and-commercial-buildings-guide-chapter-4), critic

### TS-CC-09. Repeat rule. If there are 2 or more comfort complaints (cold or hot) from the same floor and tenant in 30 days, OR opposite fixes on any zone of that floor within 30 days, switch the advice from 'quick fix' to 'find the cause' and call the FM chief engineer. Count complaints sent to the property manager by email or chat, not just helpdesk tickets. No more setpoint nudges while the flag is on.

- **Colour:** amber. **Who says yes:** FM chief engineer takes over the case.. **Section:** context. **Label:** team decision.
- **Because:** The threshold and the window are a team decision (D6), to be confirmed by a chief engineer. The sources back the idea, not the number. A study of 690 buildings found most hot and cold complaints come from poor control and HVAC faults (HC01). A thermostat change usually fixes repeat complaints only for a short time (TF-18), and the same complaints come back (PT-10, PC-18). Repeat complaints often arrive by chat or email and never become tickets (critic).
- **Applies when:** Every new comfort complaint. Tickets are joined by building, floor and tenant, with zone as a second tag.
- **Not when:** A true first complaint from that floor and tenant, with no opposite fix on any zone of the floor in the last 30 days.
- **Sources:** [HC01](https://www.aivc.org/sites/default/files/airbase_11066.pdf), [TF-18](https://aqualityhvac.org/blog/what-causes-repeated-temperature-complaints-in-commercial-spaces-with-one-thermostat-zone/), [PT-10](https://www.haroldbros.com/blog/hvac-complaints-commercial-buildings), [PC-18](https://www.haroldbros.com/blog/hvac-complaints-commercial-buildings), [HC14](https://www.facilitiesnet.com/facilitiesmanagement/article/Survey-Temperature-Complaints-Are-Most-Common-Restroom-Complaints-A-Distant-Second--14638), critic

### TS-CC-10. If the zone had a tenant fit-out in the last 12 months, pull the fit-out file. Get the endorsed AC layout plan (VAV box and thermostat locations, airflow per diffuser), the testing and commissioning report, and the air balancing report. Check that every VAV box and BMS point was switched back on and certified after the fit-out. If any of these is missing, the FM chief engineer tells the Asset Operations Manager within the week.

- **Colour:** green. **Who says yes:** No approval needed to read the file. BMS operator or FM chief engineer pulls it.. **Section:** context. **Label:** mixed.
- **Because:** In Singapore landlord fit-out rules, tenant-space VAV boxes and BMS points are cut off during fit-out and switched back on only after the landlord's contractor certifies them (TF-03, AIR-13). The tenant must submit an endorsed layout showing each VAV box and thermostat location, and an air balancing report (TF-02, SG-14, BMS-14). A missing file may be a fit-out handover gap (critic). The 12-month window is a team choice (D8).
- **Applies when:** Any comfort complaint on a floor with a tenant fit-out in the last 12 months.
- **Not when:** No layout change for a long time. The source is a CapitaLand guide, not Keppel's, so Keppel's own house rules must be checked.
- **Sources:** [TF-02](https://www.capitaland.com/content/dam/capitaland-sites/singapore/lease/commercial-spaces/welcomekit/5.%20Fitting%20Out%20Work%20Checklist%20and%20Guidelines%20(For%2021CQ)_Nov2023.pdf), [TF-03](https://www.capitaland.com/content/dam/capitaland-sites/singapore/lease/commercial-spaces/welcomekit/5.%20Fitting%20Out%20Work%20Checklist%20and%20Guidelines%20(For%2021CQ)_Nov2023.pdf), [AIR-13](https://www.capitaland.com/content/dam/capitaland-sites/singapore/lease/commercial-spaces/welcomekit/5.%20Fitting%20Out%20Work%20Checklist%20and%20Guidelines%20(For%2021CQ)_Nov2023.pdf), [SG-14](https://www.capitaland.com/content/dam/capitaland-sites/singapore/lease/commercial-spaces/welcomekit/5.%20Fitting%20Out%20Work%20Checklist%20and%20Guidelines%20(For%2021CQ)_Nov2023.pdf), [BMS-14](https://www.capitaland.com/content/dam/capitaland-sites/singapore/lease/commercial-spaces/welcomekit/5.%20Fitting%20Out%20Work%20Checklist%20and%20Guidelines%20(For%20CS)_Nov2023.pdf), [PT-16](https://www.capitaland.com/content/dam/capitaland-sites/singapore/lease/commercial-spaces/welcomekit/5.%20Fitting%20Out%20Work%20Checklist%20and%20Guidelines%20(For%20CS)_Nov2023.pdf), critic

### TS-CC-11. Before anyone changes or repairs anything installed in the fit-out (flex ducts, extra diffusers, boxes, sensors, extra cooling units), check the fit-out file. Is it landlord or tenant property? Is it still under the fit-out contractor's defects period? Tenant-owned or in-defects items go through the Asset Operations Manager to the tenant's contractor.

- **Colour:** amber. **Who says yes:** Asset Operations Manager decides the route.. **Section:** context. **Label:** critic correction.
- **Because:** After a fit-out, parts the tenant added are often the tenant's property and may still be under the fit-out contractor's defects period. If FM changes tenant work, the warranty can be lost and the blame becomes unclear (critic). Singapore fit-out practice splits the work: the tenant's contractor balances the air, and the landlord's nominated BMS contractor commissions the boxes (TF-04). Tenants restore landlord boxes and ducts using a landlord-nominated contractor (TF-06).
- **Applies when:** A repair or physical change to any item a fit-out installed or changed.
- **Not when:** Reading, measuring, or a setpoint change in the BMS. Those touch no fit-out item.
- **Sources:** critic, [TF-04](https://www.capitaland.com/content/dam/capitaland-sites/singapore/lease/commercial-spaces/welcomekit/5.%20Fitting%20Out%20Work%20Checklist%20and%20Guidelines%20(For%2021CQ)_Nov2023.pdf), [TF-06](https://www.capitaland.com/content/dam/capitaland-sites/singapore/lease/connects/2024/Plain%20English%20Tenants%20Guide%20template%20(Revised%20150124-01.pdf)

### TS-CC-12. Check the time pattern. Is the complaint outside the building's normal air-con hours? Then it is an after-hours request matter, not a fault. For cold mornings, check the start-up settings on the BMS: when pre-cooling starts, the overnight setpoint, any after-hours overrides, and morning humidity. The humidity link to cold mornings is a team inference. Changing a schedule or start-up setting needs the FM chief engineer.

- **Colour:** green. **Who says yes:** No approval needed to check. FM chief engineer approves schedule or start-up changes.. **Section:** context. **Label:** public source.
- **Because:** Singapore landlords usually run air-con only in normal office hours, and after-hours air-con is booked (BMS-18). Go 25 says to start cooling 30 to 60 minutes before staff arrive, aiming for 25 C (PC-11, SG-09, BMS-17). Systems without fresh-air pre-treatment may overcool for a while to control humidity (PC-07, SG-08). Singapore humidity is above 90% before sunrise (PC-10). Areas can take up to an hour to reach setpoint after fans are off overnight (HC13).
- **Applies when:** Complaints cluster in the first hours of the day, near the end of normal hours, or after hours.
- **Not when:** The cold lasts all day at a steady level. Then look at minimum airflow and supply air (TS-CC-15, TS-CC-16).
- **Sources:** [BMS-18](https://www.corporatelocations.com.sg/pdf/TenantsGuide.pdf), [PC-11](https://hy-me.com.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Go-25-Industry-Guide-for-Building-Operations-1.pdf), [SG-09](https://hy-me.com.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Go-25-Industry-Guide-for-Building-Operations-1.pdf), [BMS-17](https://hy-me.com.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Go-25-Industry-Guide-for-Building-Operations-1.pdf), [PC-07](https://hy-me.com.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Go-25-Industry-Guide-for-Building-Operations-1.pdf), [SG-08](https://hy-me.com.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Go-25-Industry-Guide-for-Building-Operations-1.pdf), [PC-10](https://www.weather.gov.sg/climate-climate-of-singapore/), [HC13](https://ocsi.umich.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/EnergyTip_TemperatureTroubleshooting_FINAL.pdf)

### TS-CC-13. Before reading the trend, check for leftover overrides: points in hand or manual mode, forced dampers, setpoint changes nobody can explain, controllers offline, and after-hours overrides. Also read the BMS alarm and event log for the last 2 weeks: any fire alarm test, false alarm or AHU trip. Releasing an override is a change. A stop-gap override lasts at most 7 days unless the chief engineer renews it, and the BMS operator removes expired ones at each daily or weekly review. Any reset after a fire alarm goes through the Fire Safety Manager.

- **Colour:** green. **Who says yes:** No approval needed to check. FM chief engineer approves releasing or changing an override (team decision D9). Fire Safety Manager for any reset after a fire alarm.. **Section:** remote check. **Label:** mixed.
- **Because:** PNNL found overrides left in place, equipment left in hand mode and controllers offline, and NIST found a manual override that looked like a sensor or damper fault (BMS-11). Temporary overrides are often never removed, and an unexplained one often means an earlier fault was managed, not fixed (R17). After a fire alarm test or a false alarm, AHUs and dampers sometimes stay off until someone resets them (critic). Stop-gaps with no end date are how forgotten overrides happen. The 7 days is a team choice (critic).
- **Applies when:** Always, as part of the remote check, and whenever a trend looks strange.
- **Not when:** Never skip it. If the BMS does not log setpoint changes, ask the FM team for the manual log.
- **Sources:** [BMS-11](https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/PNNL-29078.pdf), [R17](https://watsonsbs.com/blog/preventing-bms-problems-during-seasonal-changeover), [SG-10](https://hy-me.com.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Go-25-Industry-Guide-for-Building-Operations-1.pdf), [BMS-12](https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/PNNL-29078.pdf), critic

### TS-CC-14. The BMS operator pulls the trend before anyone goes to the floor. For each complaint zone: zone temperature, setpoint, damper position, airflow, airflow setpoint, occupied mode, supply air temperature and humidity. Add the AHU points (supply air temperature and setpoint, duct static pressure, fan status, cooling valve) when more than one zone complains. Put core and west zones side by side. Then plot every VAV damper on that AHU on one chart. Many boxes near fully open means look at the AHU. This is a team inference. One box near fully open all day while the others sit mid-range means look at that box, its duct and its sensor. Read the box health flags (starved, stalled).

- **Colour:** green. **Who says yes:** No approval needed. BMS operator does it.. **Section:** remote check. **Label:** public source.
- **Because:** A trend shows the pattern across days, and some faults only show over time (BMS-01). PNNL lists 'zones with comfort complaints' first among zones to trend (AIR-02). These are the points to trend (BMS-02, BMS-03), with zones on each side and in the core (BMS-04). PNNL calls it healthy when most dampers sit about 50 to 75% open, and bad when too many are near fully open or fully closed (AIR-01, BMS-05). A rogue zone can point to an undersized box, a crimped flex duct, or a bad sensor (AIR-12). Box controllers flag a starved box or a stalled damper (AIR-06). The 2-week length is a team choice. PNNL suggests 2 to 3 weeks for energy data (BMS-03).
- **Applies when:** Any comfort complaint on a floor with a BMS.
- **Not when:** The BMS does not store these points, or the floor uses fan coil units. Then note the gap, switch logging on or use small data loggers, and recheck in a few days (PT-08).

### TS-CC-15. If a low-load zone (such as the core near the lift in the morning) sits below setpoint while its VAV box is held at minimum airflow, or airflow sits well above its setpoint, flag it to the FM chief engineer. Two causes are likely: the minimum airflow is set too high, or the box airflow sensor is out of calibration. Do not lower a box minimum until the FM chief engineer checks that the zone still gets its design fresh air (SS 553), and rechecks CO2 afterwards.

- **Colour:** amber. **Who says yes:** FM chief engineer plus the controls contractor for any change to the box minimum or any recalibration.. **Section:** remote check. **Label:** mixed.
- **Because:** PNNL names these two causes of overcooling (AIR-03, TF-16). A design course calls it the 'ice box effect' in inner rooms at part occupancy (PC-12). Such zones may never warm up to the cooling setpoint at all (PT-02). Airflow above setpoint can also mean a stuck damper actuator (BMS-07). Box minimums may be set to meet ventilation design, so lowering one can cut fresh air below design (critic). A minimum set too low risks poor mixing, drafts or not enough fresh air (PC-13). CO2 is the practical stand-in for fresh air (SG-16).
- **Applies when:** The trend shows the room below setpoint while the damper will not close below a fixed value, or airflow sits well above its setpoint.
- **Not when:** The damper closes normally and the zone is still cold. Then look at supply air or drafts (TS-CC-16, TS-CC-19). Also not when nearby heat makes the sensor read wrong (TS-CC-18).
- **Sources:** [AIR-03](https://www.pnnl.gov/sites/default/files/media/file/ch7_terminal_units.pdf), [TF-16](https://www.pnnl.gov/sites/default/files/media/file/ch7_terminal_units.pdf), [PC-12](https://pdhonline.com/courses/m252/m252content.pdf), [PC-13](https://cccengineering.com.au/design-memos/vav-system-design/), [PT-02](https://escholarship.org/content/qt13s1q2xc/qt13s1q2xc_noSplash_fa5c127ab82a791c4c19c8237e7b7925.pdf?t=niiwhf), [BMS-07](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=861015), [SG-16](https://www.nea.gov.sg/our-services/public-cleanliness/environmental-cleaning-guidelines/advisories/guidance-on-improving-ventilation-and-indoor-air-quality-in-buildings-for-a-healthy-indoor-environment), critic

### TS-CC-16. Do not raise the AHU supply air temperature to cure cold complaints unless the FM chief engineer agrees after looking at the humidity trend. It changes the air for every zone on that AHU, maybe other floors too.

- **Colour:** amber. **Who says yes:** FM chief engineer.. **Section:** remote check. **Label:** public source.
- **Because:** Warmer supply air removes less moisture, so room humidity can rise. In one example it rose from 60% to 66% (AIR-16, PC-08). A flat supply air trend can mean the reset control is not working, and air that is too cold causes drafts (BMS-06). Singapore is very humid before sunrise (PC-10).
- **Applies when:** Anyone proposes raising the AHU supply air setpoint, or the supply air trend looks flat or too cold.
- **Not when:** The fix is local to one box (its minimum airflow, damper or sensor) and does not change the AHU.

### TS-CC-17. Agree access before the floor checks. The helpdesk agrees a time with the tenant contact. Never enter a boardroom during a meeting without the tenant's OK. Take an in-use CO2 reading only with the tenant's OK, or leave a small logger in the room with permission. Ceiling work over desks is done after hours, or with the area below roped off.

- **Colour:** green. **Who says yes:** No approval needed to ask. Helpdesk arranges it with the tenant contact.. **Section:** on floor check. **Label:** critic correction.

Walking into a boardroom in use, or opening the ceiling above occupied desks, is a lease and relationship risk and a falling-object risk to the people below (critic). Small loggers left in the complaint area show the real pattern over time (PT-08).
- **Applies when:** Before any check inside the tenant's space. This includes the safety look in checklist step 6.

### TS-CC-18. Before trusting a BMS zone temperature, check the sensor on the floor. Is it in the room it controls, and wired to the right box? Compare where it sits with the thermostat location on the fit-out layout plan. Is it at the height and position set in the building's design or fit-out specification, on an inside wall, away from sun, supply diffusers, return grilles and heat-giving equipment such as monitors and printers? Does it read the same as a calibrated handheld beside it? An offset is temporary and is logged with an end date. A sensor that has drifted is replaced.

- **Colour:** green. **Who says yes:** No approval needed to check. FM chief engineer approves offsets or rewiring. Moving a thermostat inside the tenant's space needs the tenant's written OK plus the Asset Operations Manager (team decision D9).. **Section:** on floor check. **Label:** mixed.
- **Because:** Go 25 asks whether thermostats were moved after layout changes, and says sensors should sit away from sun, vents and heat sources and be checked against a reference thermometer at least once a year (TF-07, TF-08, PT-14). The 1996 NEA guideline asks if thermostats are correctly placed after building changes (PC-04, SG-03). Landlord rules say the thermostat must sense the real room temperature (TF-05, SG-15). Sensors are sometimes wired to the next room's box (AIR-08). Equipment under a sensor makes it read high and overcool the room (TF-14, PT-05). A zone sensor can drift badly (BMS-08), and sensor errors were the most common VAV fault in one Hong Kong survey (AIR-18). An offset can hide a sensor that keeps drifting (critic).
- **Applies when:** After any fit-out, when the BMS and what people feel do not agree, or when one zone reads oddly next to its neighbours.
- **Not when:** The sensor has a recent calibration record, sits in the right room at a good spot, and matches a handheld reading.

### TS-CC-19. Measure what people feel where they sit. Feeling for cold drafts under diffusers is only a first screen. Then measure air speed with an anemometer at seated head and ankle height under the diffuser. For 'stuffy', take a CO2 reading at breathing height for at least 5 minutes, with the tenant's OK (TS-CC-17). Check that each closed room has a return grille or transfer path. Look for blocked or fiddled supply grilles and sweating diffusers.

- **Colour:** green. **Who says yes:** No approval needed. FM technician does it.. **Section:** on floor check. **Label:** mixed.
- **Because:** Low airflow through some diffusers lets cold air 'dump' onto people (AIR-11, PC-13). The 1996 NEA guideline keeps air movement at the workstation below 0.25 m/s (1996 NEA guideline, check current SS 554) (SG-11). NEA says SS 554:2026 sets CO2 at no more than 700 ppm above outdoor air, read for at least 5 minutes per spot at breathing height (SG-16). Tenants must provide return air grilles for partitioned rooms (TF-06). Occupants sometimes tamper with diffusers when cold (HC08, PT-12). Vents sweat when humid air meets a vent that is too cold (PC-09).
- **Applies when:** Complaints are local to some desks, a room feels stuffy when full, or water beads on a diffuser.
- **Not when:** The whole zone reads cold or hot on the BMS. Then it is an airflow or supply problem (TS-CC-14). Water from a pipe leak is a different fault and may be a red stop (TS-CC-03).

### TS-CC-20. For a hot west-facing glass room in the afternoon, suspect sun heat first. ### TS-CC-21. First-complaint nudge. On a first comfort complaint, with no repeat flag, no red stop, and no setpoint change on that zone in the last 30 days, a person may make ONE setpoint nudge of at most 1 degree C, inside the building's band. Log who asked, why, the before value and a review date. Mark the ticket 'Attended, relief given', not 'Fixed'.

- **Colour:** green. **Who says yes:** No approval needed. FM technician or BMS operator makes and logs it.. **Section:** fix. **Label:** team decision.
- **Because:** This is a team decision (D9). It gives the tenant quick relief and keeps the response fast. The 1 degree C limit and the 30-day window are team choices, not sourced numbers. The sources warn that a thermostat change alone often helps only for a while (TF-18, PT-10), so the nudge is allowed once, it is logged with its before value (HC17), and any repeat inside 30 days turns on the repeat rule (TS-CC-09).
- **Applies when:** A first comfort complaint from that floor and tenant, when the fix section is reached. In v1.0 the floor checks are not a gate for this nudge.
- **Not when:** The repeat flag is on (TS-CC-09). A red stop applies. The zone had a setpoint change in the last 30 days, or the change would be above 1 degree C (TS-CC-22). ### TS-CC-22. Any further setpoint change needs the FM chief engineer's yes first. That means a second setpoint change on the same floor within 30 days, any change above 1 degree C, or any BMS override. Record the before values, log it, and give any temporary change an end date of at most 7 days unless the chief engineer renews it.

- **Colour:** amber. **Who says yes:** FM chief engineer, logged.. **Section:** fix. **Label:** team decision.
- **Because:** This is a team decision (D9). On a shared zone, one change upsets other people (HC02). A Singapore field study found offices overcooled partly because staff lowered temperatures to stop complaints from a few spots (PC-06). During a planned slow rise towards 25 C, Go 25 advises not lowering the temperature at the first complaint (SG-01). Schedule overrides often hide deeper problems (BMS-12). One technician recorded the original values before changing them, in case they needed resetting (HC17). Stop-gaps need an end date (critic).
- **Applies when:** Anyone is about to change a setpoint, schedule or override after the first nudge, or by more than 1 degree C.
- **Not when:** The single first-complaint nudge under TS-CC-21.
- **Sources:** [HC02](https://escholarship.org/content/qt09z5423x/qt09z5423x_noSplash_467ea272070f78c6de2a425a5163b9bd.pdf), [PC-06](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2020.567787/full), [SG-01](https://hy-me.com.sg/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Go-25-Industry-Guide-for-Building-Operations-1.pdf), [BMS-12](https://www.pnnl.gov/main/publications/external/technical_reports/PNNL-29078.pdf), [HC17](https://www1.builtspace.com/2017/06/08/hot-cold-tales-hvac-trenches/), critic

### TS-CC-23. Write everything on the ticket. Record the 'before' values before any change. Close with a cause code, or 'cause not yet known' plus a review date. The 'Fixed' test and its windows are a team decision (D3).

- **Not when:** Never skipped. Read-only checks still log the location and findings.

### TS-CC-24. The FM chief engineer asks for the existing air balancing report and, if needed, a specialist for air balancing and ACMV diagnostics. ### TS-CC-25. The Asset Operations Manager decides anything about money or the commercial relationship: any cost outside the FM contract, who pays, goodwill, tenant-side works, lasting glass or shading works, any tenant message that assigns cost, offers goodwill or touches fault, and a tenant's request to run the floor colder than the building's running setpoint. ### TS-CC-26. Out of scope. Stop all setpoint advice and escalate to the FM chief engineer when the trend shows a zone's VAV damper fully open for hours on sunny afternoons (a likely capacity limit), when the cause points to any chilled-water plant setting, or when no approved rule covers the case. - **Not when:** The damper is fully open only briefly, such as during morning start-up, and the room then reaches setpoint.

- **Rule:** Zone had a tenant fit-out in the last 12 months: do not nudge the setpoint. Check where the thermostat sits against the fit-out drawing first.
- **Because:** After a fit-out, the thermostat may no longer sense the room it is meant to control, so a setpoint nudge chases the wrong room. Landlord fit-out rules require a layout plan that marks each thermostat location (TF-02) and say the thermostat must sense the real room temperature (TF-05, SG-15). Go 25 and the 1996 NEA guideline both ask whether thermostats were moved correctly after layout changes (TF-07, PT-14, PC-04, SG-03). A practitioner notes that after a buildout the zone sensors may not have been relocated (PT-03). Hot or cold areas that appear after a remodel are a sign the system no longer matches the layout (HC18). A thermostat change usually fixes repeat complaints only for a short time when the real cause is thermostat location (TF-18).

## Glossary

- **ACMV:** Air-conditioning and mechanical ventilation. The building's whole cooling and fresh-air system.
- **AHU (air handling unit):** A big fan box that cools air and pushes it through ducts to a floor or several floors.
- **Supply air temperature:** How cold the air is when it leaves the AHU.
- **Duct static pressure:** How hard the AHU pushes air through the ducts. Too low means boxes far away cannot get enough air.
- **VAV (variable air volume) box:** A small box in the ceiling that controls how much cold air goes into one zone.
- **Damper:** A flap inside a VAV box or duct that opens and closes to let more or less air through.
- **Actuator:** The small motor that moves the damper.
- **Minimum airflow:** The least air a VAV box will always send, even when the room is already cool. It may also be what brings in enough fresh air.
- **Airflow sensor / K-factor:** The part in a VAV box that measures how much air goes through, and the setting that turns its reading into real airflow. If either is off, the BMS shows the wrong airflow.
- **Pressure independent VAV / DDC controller:** A VAV box with its own small computer that measures airflow and reports it to the BMS.
- **Starved box:** A VAV box that is fully open but still cannot get the air it asks for.
- **Stalled actuator:** A damper motor that is stuck or blocked and cannot move.
- **Rogue zone:** A zone that keeps asking for maximum cooling almost all day, often because of a fault or a heat load bigger than the design.
- **Capacity limit:** When the system is already giving a zone all the air it can and the room is still too hot.
- **Hunting:** Airflow or temperature swinging up and down again and again because a control is badly tuned.
- **Reheat:** A heater in a VAV box that warms air for a room that would otherwise get too cold.
- **Zone:** An area controlled by one VAV box and one sensor.
- **Perimeter zone / core zone:** Perimeter is the area near the windows. Core is the area deep inside the floor, such as near the lifts.
- **Sensor / thermostat:** The device on the wall that measures room temperature and tells the system to cool more or less.
- **Sensor offset:** A correction set in the BMS to fix a sensor that reads a little high or low. It is only temporary.
- **Calibration / reference thermometer:** Checking a sensor against a trusted thermometer to see if it reads true.
- **Anemometer:** A small tool that measures how fast air is moving.
- **Setpoint:** The target temperature the system tries to reach.
- **Nudge:** One small setpoint change, here at most 1 degree C.
- **Running setpoint:** The setpoint the building normally runs a floor at.
- **Building's band:** The temperature range the building is set to run within.
- **BMS (building management system):** The computer system that runs and watches the building's equipment.
- **BMS operator:** The person who watches and runs the BMS from a screen.
- **Trend:** A saved record of readings over time, shown as a chart.
- **Point:** One reading or control in the BMS, such as one room temperature or one damper position.
- **Override / manual or hand mode:** A setting that forces equipment to do something whatever the automatic controls say.
- **Stop-gap:** A short, temporary change while the real fix is arranged. It needs an end date.
- **Alarm and event log:** The BMS list of alarms and events, such as a fire alarm test or an equipment trip.
- **Pre-cool / optimal start:** Starting the air-con before people arrive so the floor is cool by the start of the day.
- **Night setback:** A higher setpoint or standby mode used after hours to save energy.
- **Diffuser:** The ceiling vent that blows cold air into the room.
- **Return grille:** The vent where room air goes back to the AHU.
- **Transfer duct:** A short duct that lets air leave a closed room so it can get back to the AHU.
- **Short cycling (supply to return):** Cold air going straight from a supply vent into a return vent without cooling the room.
- **Dumping:** Cold air falling straight down from a diffuser onto people because it leaves too slowly.
- **Draft:** Moving air that makes a person feel cold.
- **Air balancing / test and balance report:** Measuring and adjusting the air at every vent so each room gets its design amount, and the report that records the results.
- **Fit-out:** The tenant's work to build out its space, such as walls, rooms, ceilings and wiring.
- **Defects period:** The time after a fit-out when the fit-out contractor must fix faults in its own work.
- **Landlord or tenant property:** Whether a part belongs to the building owner or to the tenant who put it in.
- **Nominated contractor:** A contractor the landlord chooses that tenants must use for certain work, such as BMS changes.
- **Controls contractor:** The specialist firm that changes BMS and VAV settings.
- **Qualified Person / professional engineer:** A registered engineer who must design or endorse certain building works in Singapore.
- **Tenant contact:** The person at the tenant company who the building team talks to.
- **House rules:** The building's own rules for tenants, such as whether heaters are allowed.
- **Trust signal:** A sign that a tenant has stopped relying on the building, such as a heater under a desk.
- **Solar gain:** Heat from the sun coming in through glass.
- **Radiant heat:** Heat you feel from a hot surface, like sunny glass, even when the air is not hot.
- **Low-E glass / solar film:** Glass coating or window film that keeps out some of the sun's heat.
- **External shading:** Shades outside the glass that block sun before it reaches the window.
- **Relative humidity (RH):** How much moisture is in the air compared with the most it can hold at that temperature.
- **Dew point:** The temperature at which moisture in the air turns into water drops on a cold surface.
- **Dehumidification / fresh-air pre-treatment:** Taking moisture out of the air, sometimes in a separate unit before outdoor air reaches the AHU.
- **Sweating diffuser:** A vent with water drops on it because humid air touches its cold surface.
- **CO2 / ppm:** Carbon dioxide that people breathe out, measured in parts per million. High CO2 means not enough fresh air.
- **IAQ (indoor air quality):** How clean and fresh the air inside a building is.
- **Fresh air / ventilation design:** The amount of outdoor air each zone is designed to get.
- **FCU (fan coil unit):** A smaller local unit with its own fan and cooling coil, used instead of VAV in some spaces.
- **Chiller / chilled water plant:** The central machines that make cold water for the AHUs.
- **Refrigerant:** The special gas inside chillers and air-con units that carries heat. Only certified people may handle it.
- **LEW (Licensed Electrical Worker):** A person licensed in Singapore to do or supervise electrical work.
- **Isolate, lock off, prove dead:** Switch the power off, lock it so nobody can switch it back on, and test that it is really off before touching anything.
- **Mains side:** The part of the wiring that carries full building power, not the low-voltage control side.
- **Work at height / permit-to-work:** Work where someone could fall, such as on a ladder, and the written OK that controls who may do it and how.
- **Platform ladder / mobile platform:** A ladder or stand with a flat, railed top to stand on, safer than a leaning ladder.
- **PPE:** Personal protective equipment, such as a mask, eye protection and gloves.
- **Fire damper:** A flap in a duct that shuts to stop fire spreading through a wall or floor.
- **Return-air smoke detector:** A detector in the AHU return air that shuts the AHU down when it senses smoke.
- **Fire Safety Manager:** The person appointed for a building's fire safety, who leads its fire emergency plan.
- **Fire emergency plan / fire wardens:** The building's written plan for a fire, and the trained staff who guide people out.
- **CERT:** The building's company emergency response team: trained staff who help in an emergency.
- **SCDF / 995:** The Singapore Civil Defence Force, and the number to call for fire or an ambulance.
- **CMMS:** The computer system that tracks maintenance work orders.
- **Cause code:** A required field on a work order that says why something failed, not just what was done.
- **Repeat flag:** The marker that turns on when a floor and tenant meet the repeat rule (TS-CC-09).
- **Attended, relief given:** A ticket closed after quick help. It does not mean the cause is fixed.
- **Fixed:** The cause is found and fixed, and checked over weeks. Only the building manager marks it.
- **Golden test:** A fixed test case that the rules must pass before a new version goes live.
- **FM chief engineer:** The senior engineer at the building who approves technical decisions.
- **Building manager:** The property manager's person who explains things to the tenant and marks a problem 'Fixed'.
- **Asset Operations Manager (AOM):** Keppel's manager who decides on money and tenant matters for the building.
- **Asset manager:** The Keppel manager who approves spending above the AOM's limit.
- **Go 25:** Singapore's movement, led by SGBC, to set office air-con at about 25 C, with a guide for building operators.
- **SS 553 / SS 554:** Singapore Standards for air-con design and for indoor air quality in air-conditioned buildings. Comfort numbers in this pack come from the 1996 NEA guideline, check current SS 554.
- **Green Mark:** Singapore's green building rating scheme run by BCA.
- **Occupied zone:** The part of the room where people are: floor to 1.8 m high and more than 0.6 m from the walls (1996 NEA guideline, check current SS 554).
- **m/s:** Metres per second, how fast the air moves.
- **CMH:** Cubic metres per hour, how much air flows through a vent.
- **clo:** A unit for how warm clothing is. 1.0 clo is about a business suit.
