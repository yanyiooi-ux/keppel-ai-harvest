# The Keppel challenge, in our words

Source: the official hackathon handbook (pages 9 to 13 for the challenge, 37 to 40 for submission and judging).
Every team member received the handbook PDF by email (it is not in this repo). This page is our summary, so re-check
the handbook before quoting it anywhere official.

## The challenge: "AI HARVEST: Turning Expert Know-How into Reusable Intelligence"

**The business issue.** Keppel's buildings (office towers, logistics facilities, data centres) run on the judgement
of experienced people across engineering, maintenance, energy, leasing, tenant services, sustainability, safety and
operations. That know-how is hard to pass on:

- it sits with a few experts,
- new staff take a long time to build it,
- best practice is applied unevenly,
- it walks out the door when people retire or move on.

**The question.** How might an organisation systematically turn human expertise into reusable intelligence that
supports decisions, operations and continuous improvement across a portfolio of assets?

**What they want built.** A platform and operating model that turns expert know-how into reusable, governed
capabilities they call **"Intelligence Pills"**. A pill covers one area of expertise and can hold knowledge,
rules of thumb, decision logic, guardrails and escalation rules. Teams should focus on one scenario from these pill
areas: Asset Operations, Energy Optimisation, Leasing, Technical Services, Sustainability, Tenant Experience.

The track has one challenge. Inside it we pick one scenario from the six pill areas above, and we name that scenario
in the first minute of the presentation and the demo.

## What the solution should do

1. **Capture** know-how from experienced people, including knowledge that is not written down anywhere.
2. **Structure** it into a reusable, governed capability: context, decision logic, guardrails, an owner, approval rules.
3. **Apply** it through one or more specialist AI agents or workflows (a pill does not have to equal one agent).
4. **Coordinate** across domains when an issue spans more than one area.
5. **Keep people in control**: people guide, supervise and review the AI and its recommendations.
6. **Learn through governed feedback**: outcomes, expert corrections and new experience are reviewed, validated,
   versioned and approved before they are used.

## Qualities the solution should have

Human-supervised, transparent (you can see what it relied on), accountable (who owns it), reliable (how
recommendations are checked), reusable, scalable (more asset types and areas), governed, portable (Keppel owns the
knowledge and can move it to another vendor or model), version-controlled (with rollback), secure (access by role
and sensitivity), and clearly bounded (what it may recommend or do, what needs human approval, what must be escalated).

## The solution diagram must answer these 8 questions

1. What specific business problem is being addressed?
2. Whose expertise is captured, and how is knowledge that is not already documented obtained?
3. How does the captured expertise become reusable, rather than a one-off answer?
4. How would an Asset Operations Manager use and supervise it, check its recommendations, and know when a human
   must step in?
5. Why is it more than a chatbot or a search tool?
6. How are feedback, outcome data and new expertise reviewed, validated, versioned and approved after deployment?
7. How could it extend from one asset or domain to many assets, business units or the whole enterprise?
8. What is a credible path to build it with current or near-term technology?

Also required for this track: a **live demo walkthrough** and the **complete source code as a GitHub repository**.
The handbook says these features are guidance only and alternative approaches are welcome.

## Judging

**Preliminary round** (decides the 2 finalists per track): judged by Keppel and Tencent Cloud experts on detailed
track criteria that the organisers share after registration. Save them in
[`track-criteria.md`](track-criteria.md) when they arrive.

**Demo Day** (finalists only): the handbook lists these ten criteria, 10 points each:

| Criterion | What they look at |
|---|---|
| Impact and Relevance | A real problem, meaningful value |
| Human-Centered Design | Designed for real users |
| AI Interaction | Quality and depth of AI use |
| Technical Execution | Technical quality and completeness |
| Feasibility | Realistic and scalable beyond the hackathon |
| Demo and Storytelling | How well it is presented |
| Innovation and Creativity | Originality |
| UX and Accessibility | Usability and accessibility |
| Responsible AI and Ethics | Trustworthy, responsible AI practice |
| Overall Quality | Judges' overall impression |
